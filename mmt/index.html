Build and run evals

Define frustration scores

Every point has a cause

Repeats, wrong picks, angry words and fee disputes add points; progress removes them. At 50 the bot offers a person, at 75 it hands over.

AI understands, rules decide

Gemini reads what the caller says. Fixed rules control access, fees, refunds and every action, so the model never cancels anything by itself.

No action without a clear yes

The bot reads back the fee and refund, and re-checks the price right before acting. If it changed, it asks again.

Role-based access

An employee sees only their own bookings; a central booker can manage the team's.

Quality gates before scale

Releases are judged on the eval set, with critical errors (a wrong cancellation, someone else's booking shown) held at zero.

If I joined

What I'd look at in my first weeks

  1. Which post-booking intents drive the most contacts, and which the bot can resolve safely end to end.
  2. Where cancellation, date change and refund journeys lose customers to a call, measured as contacts per booking.
  3. How handoffs split between "the bot couldn't" and "the bot shouldn't", since only the first group should shrink.
Honest gap: my bot runs in the browser on sample bookings, not on a phone line or a live GDS. Real deployment needs telephony, low latency, interruption handling and booking-system integration.
