Case study

Network Watch: parcel-network disruption control tower

Detects a disruption, estimates how many parcels are at risk, compares every recovery plan and recommends one for a manager to approve.

Problem

When fog, rain, a closed road or an overloaded sort centre hits a parcel network, a manager has to decide fast which parcels are at risk, what to do and what each option costs, using scattered views and judgment.

Users

Network and hub operations managers, line-haul planners, and the people who own delivery promises to customers.

My role

Owner of the product and PRD (v2.0): scoped the problem, defined the decision flow and requirements, and built it with AI-assisted development.

Key decisions

  • One connected flow: detect → quantify → optimise → recommend → explain and approve.
  • Combine four responses (divert, temporary capacity, prioritise urgent parcels, change promises) instead of single fixes; a min-cost-flow optimiser compares all 16 combinations.
  • Planner in the loop: the tool proposes; a manager approves or rejects with a note, and the decision is stored.
  • The Gemini copilot answers only from the engine's own numbers, with sources and timestamps, in English or Hinglish.

Known gaps

  • Network, volumes and costs are sample data; outcomes are model estimates, not real delayed-parcel counts.
  • Validating it needs historical shipment volumes, promise vs. actual delivery times and real intervention costs.

Tools

TomTom truck routing with live traffic, Open-Meteo weather, Gemini function calling, Supabase, Next.js, Vercel.
